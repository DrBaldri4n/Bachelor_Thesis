\subsection{Research Questions}

The focus of this thesis is on the development and application of "Artificial Intelligence-based Anomaly Detection for Electric Vehicle Supply Equipment using Long Short-Term Memory Architecture". To provide a structured approach to this investigation, the following research questions have been formulated:

\begin{itemize}
    \item How can data acquisition and preprocessing, including event selection strategies, sampling intervals, and feature aggregation, be optimized to enable high detection accuracy and real-time anomaly detection in resource-constrained embedded EVSE systems?
    \item What are the key limitations, uncertainties, and open challenges in transferring, deploying, and operating AI-based anomaly detection models in heterogeneous and distributed electric vehicle charging infrastructures?
    \item How can the explainability and transparency of LSTM-based anomaly detection systems for Electric Vehicle Supply Equipment (EVSE) be enhanced to increase trust and acceptance among users and operators?
\end{itemize}

By addressing these questions, this thesis aims to provide insights into the technical implementation and practical deployment of anomaly detection systems in EVSE environments, establishing a foundation for the subsequent analysis and discussion chapters.
