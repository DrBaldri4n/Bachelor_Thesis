\subsection{Structure of the Thesis}

This thesis is divided into ten chapters, each building upon the previous one to explore the use of AI-based methods—specifically Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architectures—for anomaly detection in Electric Vehicle Supply Equipment (EVSE).\\

The introduction presents the motivation and relevance of the topic, outlines the role of anomaly detection in the context of EV infrastructure, and formulates the central research questions. Additionally, it provides an overview of prior AI-based approaches and concludes with the structural outline of the work. The theoretical foundations necessary for the thesis are summarized, including the basics of anomaly detection, a short introduction to tree-based models such as Decision Trees and Isolation-Forest, and a focused explanation of the LSTM architecture. Related work is presented, covering research on security aspects and anomaly detection mechanisms specific to EVSE, as well as a broader review of AI-based detection methods applied in similar domains. The dataset creation and acquisition process is described, detailing both the CICEVSE2024 datasets and custom setups involving Prometheus monitoring tools. An exploratory data analysis (EDA) follows, intended to uncover key patterns and differences between the CICEVSE2024 and self-generated datasets. The process of data preprocessing and feature selection is discussed, including the identification of relevant features and the use of feature importance methods such as Decision Trees. The LSTM model is described, including its architecture, the training process, and the chosen strategies for hyperparameter tuning. The results of the approach are evaluated, outlining the experimental setup, evaluation metrics, and key outcomes of the applied anomaly detection system. A detailed discussion of the findings in relation to the research questions highlights limitations and potential practical applications. Finally, the work concludes with a reflection on future directions of this research.\\

This structure ensures a logical and methodical development of the topic, from theoretical foundations to implementation, evaluation, and critical interpretation.
