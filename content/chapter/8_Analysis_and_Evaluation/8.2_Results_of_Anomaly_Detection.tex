\paragraph{Attack detection confidence analysis.}
Figure~\ref{fig:attack_prob_analysis} shows the average \texttt{attack\_prob} values for different attack types, as estimated by the classifier, in comparison to the average value observed during benign traffic. The vertical dashed line represents the benign baseline (approximately 0.1614). \\
Flooding attacks such as \textit{ICMP flood}, \textit{UDP flood}, and \textit{TCP flood} exhibit clearly distinguishable \texttt{attack\_prob} values, often well above 0.25, with \textit{ICMP flood} peaking around 0.36. These high values indicate strong model confidence and allow for reliable detection.\\
In contrast, attacks like \textit{Slowloris}, \textit{Aggressive Scan}, \textit{Portscan}, and \textit{OS Fingerprinting} present significantly lower \texttt{attack\_prob} values, frequently falling below or very close to the benign average. This suggests that these attacks are more likely to be misclassified or overlooked, highlighting a potential weakness in the current detection model.\\
These results align with real-world test observations, where slower and stealthier attacks blend more seamlessly with normal traffic patterns, making them more challenging to detect. Future work should explore enhanced feature extraction or anomaly-based methods to improve classification confidence for these low-profile attacks.
